# Recruiter Evaluation Checklist

Recruiters spend **6–10 seconds** on a first pass. This checklist mirrors what
they look for in that scan. Use it during step 5 (detect weaknesses) and again
after step 10 (final review).

The order matters — recruiters read top-to-bottom, left-to-right, and stop
as soon as they have an answer.

---

## The 6-second scan (in order)

1. **Name and current title** — visible without scrolling?
2. **Most recent role** — company, title, dates, in that order?
3. **Tenure pattern** — any unexplained gaps, or job-hops shorter than 12
   months without context?
4. **Tools at a glance** — does the skills section or first bullet of the
   current role surface the 3–5 tools the role asks for?
5. **One signal of impact** — any number, scope, or outcome above the fold?
6. **Length sanity** — one page for early-career, two for mid/senior. Not
   three.

If a recruiter cannot answer all six in 6 seconds, the resume fails the
first pass, regardless of how strong the content is on page 2.

---

## The 30-second second pass

Once the resume survives the 6-second scan, the recruiter reads bullets.
They check:

- [ ] Each bullet starts with an action verb (no "Responsible for").
- [ ] Each bullet says what *you* did, not what your team did.
- [ ] Bullets escalate from senior to junior detail (impact first, then how).
- [ ] No bullet is longer than 2 lines.
- [ ] No bullet is purely a tool list ("Used React, Redux, TypeScript.").
- [ ] Quantified outcomes appear in **at least every other** bullet for
  current and most-recent roles.

---

## The recruiter's red flags

These cause an immediate "no":

- [ ] Typos in name, employer, or current title.
- [ ] Email is `<name>@<current-employer>.com` (they will not contact you
      via your employer's domain).
- [ ] Mismatched tense ("Lead engineer on…" for a past role).
- [ ] Job titles that don't match LinkedIn or that don't exist
      ("Lead Senior Principal Architect II").
- [ ] Skills section claims something with no supporting bullet.
- [ ] Dates that overlap suspiciously without explanation.
- [ ] Pages 2+ that exist only to repeat page 1.

When a red flag is present, **fix it before tailoring or scoring**. A
tailored resume with a red flag is still rejected.

---

## The role-fit pass

Recruiters then compare against the job description. They ask:

- [ ] Does this resume mention **the role's title** (or a clear synonym)?
- [ ] Do the top 5 skills in the JD appear in the resume?
- [ ] Is the most-recent role *recognizable* as preparation for the target?
- [ ] Does the candidate's seniority match the target (within ±1 level)?
- [ ] If remote/onsite/hybrid is specified, is the candidate's location
      compatible?

A mismatch here is not a red flag — it's a tailoring opportunity. Surface it
in the report's "gap analysis" section.

---

## The "would I forward this?" test

After all the above, the recruiter asks themselves: would I forward this to
the hiring manager? If the answer needs a justification longer than one
sentence, the resume is not ready.

The skill's job is to produce a resume that earns the forward in one
sentence: "Strong $ROLE candidate at $LEVEL with shipped $SIGNAL."
